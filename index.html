BEHIND THE SKY

An expanding particle universe.

Each source has a 3D position, constant intrinsic luminosity and a velocity relative to the expanding background. A comoving observer sits inside the volume. Numerical integration of the Planck ΛCDM expansion determines the particles’ physical positions; their distances determine brightness by the inverse-square law. Today’s directions and apparent magnitudes match Tycho-2.

This is a controlled experiment with freely moving luminous tracers. Tycho supplies directions and magnitudes, but this subset supplies no distances or velocities. Those are assumed, so the early view is a simulation, not a reconstruction of the ancient sky. Real stars in a gravitationally bound galaxy do not follow Hubble expansion.

Particle dynamics

Physical position is r = ax. For non-interacting particles, the canonical momentum p = a² dx/dt is conserved; peculiar velocity is v = p/a. The simulator numerically integrates J(a) = ∫ₜ⁽ᵃ⁾ᵗ⁰ dt/a², then evolves each particle as x(a) = x₀ − pJ(a). These are 3D trajectories in an expanding background, with no angular interpolation or formation patches. The coordinate and momentum definitions follow [7].

The endpoint priors are log-uniform distances from 100 to 1,500 parsecs and isotropic Gaussian peculiar velocities with a present-day dispersion of 0.002 km/s per component. These chosen values give modest angular drift over cosmic time; they are not measured stellar kinematics. The observer remains comoving at the origin. With relative motion disabled, every separation scales exactly as a and all angles stay fixed. With motion enabled, apparent separations can increase or decrease, as determined by the projected particle trajectories.

Luminosities are calibrated to each measured Tycho apparent magnitude at its assumed present distance. Flux then follows F(a)/F₀ = (d₀/d(a))². The extended catalogue contains 329,281 observed sources. Sources with present-day magnitudes between 9 and 10 become detectable when their computed apparent magnitude crosses the display’s magnitude-9 limit. Today shows the original 120,530-source selection. The external view uses the same particles and luminosities, seen from a displaced camera.

This is a non-interacting particle simulation, not a gravity or galaxy-formation solver. It omits mutual gravity, stellar evolution and death, dust, and a past light cone. The volume is local (parsecs), so the display uses Euclidean inverse-square flux rather than a cosmological luminosity-distance law. The cosmic age does not imply that every tracer is an actual surviving primordial star. Colours and the display’s exposure response are illustrative.

Primordial light and first stars

At recombination a nearly uniform warm thermal glow fills the visible sky. As it cools, the visible dark-age sky becomes dark, until sources are switched on. The phase ordering follows the physical history outlined by ESA / Planck [8] and the first-galaxies review [4]; these sources do not validate our chosen population or velocities.

The optional WMAP nine-year ILC overlay is observed microwave temperature data, reprojected from Galactic coordinates [1]. It enhances ΔT over ±200 µK and fades by two million years. Its colours are not what eyes would see. A 2D temperature map cannot uniquely determine 3D local star trajectories, so it does not control particle positions.

Cosmology & catalogue

The expansion uses Planck 2018 base-ΛCDM values H₀ = 67.4 km/s/Mpc and Ωm = 0.315 [2]. The simplified flat model includes Ωr = 0.000092 and integrates the Friedmann equation to find cosmic age. The extended Tycho-2 VT < 10 subset comes from WISC/AIDA, including both supplements [5].
