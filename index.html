BEHIND THE SKY

A sky to think with.

This is a first-order visual experiment, using today’s Tycho-2 sky as a template for earlier epochs. These are not the actual ancient positions or populations of the Milky Way’s stars.

The expansion approximation

Imagine a fixed set of sources moving with the expansion. Scale their mean separation by a, density by a⁻³, and flux by a⁻². Isotropic scaling preserves angles, so the catalogue pattern stays fixed.

Gravitationally bound stars do not follow cosmic expansion. This toy flux law omits stellar evolution, cosmological dimming, dust, and a true past light cone. Tycho VT magnitudes are measured; star colours are illustrative because this subset has no colour data. There are no stars before first light unless you turn off population evolution.

Cosmology & catalogue

A flat ΛCDM model: H₀ = 67.4 km/s/Mpc, Ωm = 0.315, Ωr = 0.000092. Cosmic age is integrated from the Friedmann equation, including radiation. The 42,072-star Tycho-2 VT < 8 subset comes from WISC/AIDA, including both supplements.
