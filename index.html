BEHIND THE SKY

A sky to think with.

This is a first-order visual experiment, using today’s Tycho-2 sky as a template for earlier epochs. The 3D view assigns illustrative depths to Tycho directions and adds 900 procedural galaxy tracers. These are not measured galaxy positions or the actual ancient populations of the Milky Way’s stars.

The expansion approximation

Imagine a fixed set of sources moving with the expansion. Scale their mean separation by a, density by a⁻³, and flux by a⁻². After the formation morph, every 3D tracer follows x(t) = a(t) x₀ with a fixed external camera, so separation is visible. Smooth transitions and playback animate that motion. The Sky view places the observer at the origin; isotropic scaling then preserves angles.

Gravitationally bound stars and galaxies do not follow cosmic expansion. This model expands all tracers together after the illustrative formation phase. The toy flux law omits stellar evolution, cosmological dimming, dust, and a true past light cone. Tycho VT magnitudes are measured; star colours are illustrative because this subset has no colour data. Coloured early points represent schematic matter, not stars.

Observed seeds, illustrative evolution

The coloured seed field is the observed WMAP nine-year ILC temperature map, in Galactic coordinates, reprojected into the viewing direction [1]. The display enhances ΔT over ±200 µK; these are microwave temperature differences, not visible colours or a direct matter-density measurement.

WMAP angular patches seed a repeatable 3D morph into diffuse matter, stellar tracers, and galaxy groups. The animation illustrates growth from primordial fluctuations [4]; it is not an N-body calculation, nor a reconstruction of which observed patch formed which galaxy. The shell-to-volume projection, cluster depths, collapse paths, and spiral glyphs are artistic choices.

Cosmology & catalogue

The expansion uses Planck 2018 base-ΛCDM values H₀ = 67.4 km/s/Mpc and Ωm = 0.315 [2]. The simplified flat model includes Ωr = 0.000092 and integrates the Friedmann equation to find cosmic age. The 120,530-star Tycho-2 VT < 9 subset comes from WISC/AIDA, including both supplements [5].
