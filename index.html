BEHIND THE SKY

A sky to think with.

The default planet sky view evolves toward the measured Tycho-2 directions and VT magnitudes. At a = 1 it is the present-day catalogue sky seen from a hypothetical planet, without synthetic clusters or galaxy sprites. Rewind to see the observed WMAP microwave field fade into darkness, followed by individual star births. Stars emerge in patches distributed around the observer and move apart as a grows, ending exactly at Tycho. Earlier populations and angular motion are visual approximations.

The optional external 3D model assigns illustrative depths and clustered positions to stellar tracers and adds 900 galaxy tracers. It demonstrates expansion from an external camera; it is not the sky seen from Earth. “Today” returns to the Tycho sky.

The expansion approximation

Imagine a fixed set of sources moving with the expansion. Scale their mean separation by a, density by a⁻³, and flux by a⁻². After the formation morph, every 3D tracer follows x(t) = a(t) x₀ with a fixed external camera, so separation is visible. Smooth transitions and playback animate that motion. Uniform physical expansion preserves angles for an observer at the origin. To show the requested increase in apparent separation, the planet view adds a phenomenological angular expansion: 1,536 irregular patches cover the whole sphere, displaced by WMAP temperature variations and deterministic jitter. Each star follows a great-circle path from its patch toward its measured Tycho direction. Angular offset from the patch scales as 0.12 + 0.88aβ, with patch-dependent β between 0.3 and 1.5 to vary the dispersal rate; at a = 1 the catalogue vector is used directly. This is not a physical prediction that bound stars expand.

Gravitationally bound stars and galaxies do not follow cosmic expansion. This model expands all tracers together after the illustrative formation phase. The toy flux law omits stellar evolution, cosmological dimming, dust, and a true past light cone. Tycho VT magnitudes are measured; star colours are illustrative because this subset has no colour data. Diffuse coloured formation points appear only after the first-star epoch; the dark ages contain no visible stellar or matter emission.

Observed seeds, illustrative evolution

The coloured seed field is the observed WMAP nine-year ILC temperature map, in Galactic coordinates, reprojected into the viewing direction [1]. The display enhances ΔT over ±200 µK; these are microwave temperature differences, not visible colours or a direct matter-density measurement.

WMAP temperature variations seed distributed formation patches in the planet view and clustered tracers in the optional external model. The microwave false-colour map fades by two million years; the visible dark-age sky stays dark until the first stars appear. The animation illustrates growth from primordial fluctuations [4]; it is not an N-body calculation, nor a reconstruction of which observed patch formed which galaxy. The shell-to-volume projection, cluster depths, collapse paths, and galaxy glyphs are artistic choices.

Cosmology & catalogue

The expansion uses Planck 2018 base-ΛCDM values H₀ = 67.4 km/s/Mpc and Ωm = 0.315 [2]. The simplified flat model includes Ωr = 0.000092 and integrates the Friedmann equation to find cosmic age. The 120,530-star Tycho-2 VT < 9 subset comes from WISC/AIDA, including both supplements [5].
